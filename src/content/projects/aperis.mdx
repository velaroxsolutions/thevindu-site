## The problem

Every dating app has converged on the same interface: a photo, a swipe, a queue.
It’s optimised for throughput. Rejecting a hundred people is faster than considering three.

So appearance becomes the entire first filter. Everything else about a person only gets
looked at after they’ve passed a two-second visual gate.

I wanted to know what happens if you remove the gate.

## What I built

You answer a set of scenario questions. The system builds a profile vector and introduces
you to people whose vectors sit close on the dimensions you weighted highest. Photos unlock
once a conversation is running.

No queue, no swipe. A small number of introductions, and the scarcity is the point.

## What I learned

The engineering was never the constraint. I could build features faster than I could find
people to use them, so for a long stretch I was optimising something nobody had touched.

What moved was messaging individuals instead of posting into the void. Slower, and the only
thing that produced real feedback.

## Next

Grow the beta until the matching has something to work with, then replace the hand-tuned
weights with something learned from real outcomes.
